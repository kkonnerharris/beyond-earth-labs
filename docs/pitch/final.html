THE PROBLEM AND THE PRODUCT

A crew leaving the habitat has three questions. The map answers each.

01 · PLAN

Where can the crew go?

A route on NASA elevation for a crew rover, a scout rover or an astronaut on foot, kept under the slope each can cross.

02 · CHECK

Can it get back safely?

Oxygen or battery for the whole trip with a reserve. A plan that does not fit is refused, and the shortfall is shown.

03 · RUN

What if the plan breaks?

The traverse runs as a simulation. An obstacle, a dust storm, a suit leak or a health alert forces a new plan, and the log says why.

One mission runs through seven connected parts. Each one hands something to the next.

BEYOND EARTH LABS02 / 07

THE MAP, CHECKED AT 1 METRE

Five NASA missions on one map. Planned at 20 m, checked at 1 m.

Layers. Orbital photographs at 6 m and 25 cm, elevation at 20 m and 1 m, thermal infrared, and Perseverance’s real path in white. Every number is tagged NASA data, calculated, model, simulated or assumption.

Checked against the real rover. The map’s elevation agrees with Perseverance’s record at 464 waypoints to a median of 1.0 m. The rover never crossed more than 18.3°, so rovers here are held to 20°.

A 20 m plan cannot see a bank a few metres wide. NASA’s 1 m model can. On the traverse shown:

9.5°

Steepest ground the 20 m plan reports.

152 m

What the 1 m model finds over the limit: nine stretches, up to 41.8°.

0 m

After the route is moved in two replans. Steepest point 18.0°, and 656 m longer.

Not every route can be cleared. To the top of the delta, 46 m of steep ground remains out of 258 m, and the check says so.

The mission map after the 1 m check. Seven steps on the left carry one mission from the map to a review. Orange is the moved route, red is the ground the 1 m model shows as too steep, white is Perseverance’s real path.

BEYOND EARTH LABS04 / 07

THE 3D SIMULATION · EARTH

How a test on Earth could help before anyone goes.

Mars hardware and procedures are tried on Earth first. The same rover, astronaut and planner run at Garden of the Gods in Shawnee National Forest, Illinois, on USGS elevation and a real aerial photograph. On ground a person can walk, a test could show:

Whether the predictions hold. Walk or drive a planned route and compare the time and energy with what the planner said.

Whether the limits are right. Take the 20° rule to a real slope, and see what the map missed: rocks, roots and loose ground smaller than a metre.

Whether the rules work under pressure. Rehearse the turn-back for a leak or a storm, with the way home always covered.

No field test has been run yet. This site is a practice ground near home, not one the network ranked, and Earth’s gravity and forest are not Mars.

The rover at Garden of the Gods. The ground is the USDA aerial photograph of 18 August 2023; trees stand where it shows forest and are painted with it. Tree shapes, close-up ground detail and the sky are illustrative.

BEYOND EARTH LABS05 / 07

DEEP LEARNING AND AI USE

A terrain network finds Earth sites that resemble Mars.

A vision transformer with a head trained on 4,534 terrain cells from three worlds ranks 29 Earth regions against any Mars or Moon region. Every answer is checked against measured elevation.

0.64

Mars regions it never saw. It explains 64% of the variation in slope, relief and roughness. Classical methods reach 52%, a simple linear model 60%.

52%

Matching Earth terrain to Mars. It closes 52% of the gap between a random pick and the best possible pick. Untrained, 17%.

0.39

Weakest on Earth. Vegetation hides the landform. An Earth-only specialist explains 39% on Earth regions it never saw; the shared network, 31%.

The network only ranks rehearsal sites. Routes never depend on it.

The top match for Jezero Crater is the Mojave volcanic fields: the network’s score and the measured score, side by side.

HOW AI WAS USED

To build it. Claude (Anthropic), through Claude Code, for project scaffolding, for choosing and testing which NASA data to use, and as a coding assistant on the code, tests and documents.

Inside the product. DINOv2 as a frozen image encoder, and the small terrain network trained by this project on NASA data.

Not used for numbers. No language model produces any figure shown. Routes, risks and budgets come from documented calculations.

Full record: docs/AI_USE.md in the repository.

WORKS TODAY

  • All seven steps run end to end; the 3D simulation runs the mission on Mars, and a rover and astronaut on Earth.
  • 112 unit tests and 27 end-to-end checks pass; a 24-screen walkthrough runs with no errors.

KNOWN LIMITS

  • Single rocks and ripples are invisible, even at 1 m. Only 2 km around the landing site is detailed in 3D.
  • The traverse and crew health are simulated. Some life-support, suit and radiation figures are assumptions, flagged. No field test on Earth has been run.
